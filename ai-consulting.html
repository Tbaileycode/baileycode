Common questions

Is it safe to put participant or client information into ChatGPT?

Not by default. Identifiable client information should not go into a consumer AI tool unless you have checked the tool's data terms, where the data is stored, whether it is used to train models, and that your privacy obligations and consent arrangements allow it. A written AI policy sets these rules once so staff are not guessing. This is general guidance, not legal advice.

Does a small provider really need an AI policy?

If staff are using AI tools, formally or not, yes. A short written policy that says which tools are approved, what information must never be entered, and who checks AI output before it reaches a client is the single most useful first step, and it can be done in days, not months.

What does an AI audit involve?

A structured look at where AI is already being used (including by staff on their own), where it could genuinely help, and where it would put privacy, duty of care or compliance at risk. You get a plain-language report and a prioritised plan, not a slide deck of buzzwords.

Can AI write NDIS reports, progress notes or support plans?

It can draft, summarise and tidy language, but a qualified person must review and own every document. The aim is to save worker time on the writing, not to remove human judgement from what is said about a person's life.
