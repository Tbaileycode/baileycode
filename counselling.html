Who counselling is for

Adults working through addiction and recovery, a late ADHD diagnosis, trauma or CPTSD, or the quiet work of figuring out who they are underneath the survival patterns. Including people who look like they are coping, and are not.

Why it's called Bailey Code

Everyone runs on a kind of code: the beliefs they absorbed, the rules they were handed, the survival patterns that once kept them safe and now keep them stuck. Behaviour that looks like the problem usually makes sense once you can see the code underneath it.

That is the idea behind the name, and behind the tagline: figuring out the code to finding you. As a counsellor, my job is not to fix you or hand you a script. It is to understand the person in front of me: what is going on for them, how they came to run the way they do, and what they want to keep or change. You are the expert on your life. I help you read it.

What that looks like in the room

  • I listen first. Before any technique, I want to understand you.
  • Curiosity, not judgement. The pattern you are ashamed of almost always made sense when it started.
  • We name the patterns together. Plain language, no jargon you have to decode.
  • You decide what changes. Nothing is done to you. You keep what works and rewrite what does not.
  • Trauma-informed pace. Safety, choice and consent come before depth.

I will always be honest about what counselling can and cannot do.

Lived experience, and formal training

I have lived experience of addiction, recovery, trauma and a late ADHD diagnosis, and I am backed by formal counselling training. The lived experience is not a replacement for the training. It is the reason I am doing it. I will not use my story as a substitute for good practice, and I will not make your session about me.

Common questions

Are counselling sessions available now?

Not yet. I hold a Graduate Certificate of Counselling from Torrens University Australia and I am completing the Masters of Counselling. Sessions will open once I am qualified. You can join the waitlist by email.

What will you work with people on?

Addiction and recovery, late ADHD diagnosis, trauma and CPTSD, identity and self-reclamation, men's work, and the overwhelm that high-functioning people carry quietly.

What does trauma-informed mean?

It means the work is built around safety, choice and trust. You set the pace, you are never pushed to share more than you want to, and nothing is done to you without your consent.

Can you diagnose ADHD, or prescribe medication?

No. An ADHD diagnosis comes from a qualified clinician such as a psychiatrist, and medication is prescribed by a doctor. Counselling can support you through assessment, diagnosis and what comes after.

Is this a crisis service?

No. If you are in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, call Lifeline on 13 11 14, or 000 in an emergency.

Do you also do hands-on support work?

Yes, as a separate service. Support work helps people with mental health conditions, including NDIS participants, with practical recovery: routines, daily living and community participation. See the support work page for details.

Do you offer coaching as well?

Yes, coaching for people dealing with overwhelm, burnout and getting unstuck. Counselling and coaching are different, and I will be clear with you about which one you are getting.
