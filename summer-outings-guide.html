Running all summer

  • Half-price public transport until 1 January 2027: the concession daily cap is $2.85 and full fare is $5.70. Under 18s travel free with a Youth myki, and Seniors Card holders travel free on weekends. City trams are free inside the Free Tram Zone.
  • Zoos Victoria: under 16s enter free on weekends, public holidays and school holidays, which covers most of summer. A concession membership is $112 a year ($9.33 a month) for unlimited entry to all four zoos.
  • Australian Open Opening Week, 11 to 16 Jan: adults $10, children $5.
  • Village Cinemas Sensory Friendly Films: $6 with a free Vrewards membership.
  • Hoyts Cheap Tuesday: $13.50 standard tickets all day.
  • Lido Cinemas, Hawthorn: Tuesday tickets $12, or $8 for members.
  • Morning Melodies at Hamer Hall: groups of four or more pay $28 each instead of $36. Group bookings open 24 Nov.
  • Book online in advance: SEA LIFE is from $39 online on a weekday against $53 on the day.

Cards that unlock ongoing discounts

  • Companion Card: free second ticket for the support worker at affiliated venues, and free public transport for them when travelling with the participant.
  • Victorian Carer Card (We Care): for unpaid family carers, not paid support workers. Worth telling families about: half-price weekday and free weekend public transport, and concession entry at Zoos Victoria and Museums Victoria.
  • Seniors Card: for participants aged 60 and over, cheaper entry at many venues (for example Melbourne Museum $12 and Zoos Victoria from $52.50).

Not confirmed for this summer: Luna Park's Summer Season Pass ($79 last year, sold in December) and any SEA LIFE Black Friday discount on single-day tickets.

Free all summer

These need no fixed date and suit short-notice or hot-day planning.

Indoors (good above 30 degrees)

  • NGV International and NGV Australia: free collection entry. Free shows this summer include Triennial (from 13 Dec), Plant Life (to 7 Feb) and Passage: The Art of Atong Atem (to 14 Jun).
  • ACMI, Fed Square: free interactive exhibition with playable games, daily 10am to 5pm. Relaxed, low-sensory visits and Auslan tours are offered.
  • Melbourne Museum and Scienceworks: free with a concession card.
  • Boroondara Summer Salon, Town Hall Gallery, Hawthorn: free, to 23 Jan.
  • Local libraries: free, cool, with quiet spaces and holiday programs. Check the council's what's-on page.

Accessible beaches

  • St Kilda, Port Melbourne and Elwood: beach matting at St Kilda and Port Melbourne Life Saving Clubs and Elwood Sailing Club. A Mobi-chair floating beach wheelchair can be booked in summer at St Kilda and Port Melbourne. Matting comes up on Monday afternoons for cleaning.
  • Williamstown: matting to the shoreline seven days a week, free beach wheelchairs on weekends in patrol hours (12 to 5pm).
  • Altona, Mordialloc, Hampton and Carrum: matting or beach wheelchairs through the local life saving clubs. Phone ahead to confirm.

Outdoors

  • Royal Botanic Gardens Melbourne: free entry, shaded paths, wheelchair hire available.
  • Christmas lights walk in the CBD (to 25 Dec) and Myer Windows (to 5 Jan).
  • Queen Vic Market Summer Night Market, Wednesdays from 6 Jan: free entry, pay for food.

Access

Autism and ADHD specific

These are quieter sessions, or social groups built around a shared interest, which tend to work better than general crowds.

Low sensory sessions

  • Barbie: The Exhibition lower sensory sessions at Melbourne Museum (dates in the museum table above).
  • Scienceworks Low Sensory Sessions, first Saturday of each month.
  • Village Sensory Friendly Films and Hoyts Sensory Screenings.
  • ACMI relaxed visits. The Christmas Magic Mirror at Southgate is listed as low sensory.
  • Low sensory climbing at Activate by Hardrock: first Sunday of the month, 4pm, no birthday parties during the session. Confirm the venue location and price.

Movement and regulation (useful for ADHD)

  • MindClimb at Clip 'n Climb Berwick: climbing program for people with ASD and ADHD, overseen by an occupational therapist, listed as suitable for NDIS funding.
  • Go karting, trampoline parks, swimming and beach days all give strong physical input in short bursts. Go off-peak (weekday mornings in term, or first session of the day in holidays).
  • Short, active outings with a clear end usually work better than long seated events. Opening Week at the tennis or a Sheffield Shield day lets people come and go.

Planning

Hot days and total fire ban days

Melbourne had several days above 40 degrees and statewide total fire bans last January. Have an indoor plan ready for every outdoor outing.

Check the night before

  • Total fire bans are usually published on the CFA website by 5pm the day before.
  • The VicEmergency app shows heat, fire and storm warnings. The Bureau of Meteorology issues heatwave warnings.

What closes

  • Parks Victoria can close parks on extreme days. Dandenong Ranges National Park has been closed on past total fire ban days, which affects Sherbrooke and TrailRider bookings.
  • Outdoor attractions can shut. Geelong's Carousel closed on total fire ban days last January, while council pools stayed open.
  • Check Puffing Billy, outdoor cinemas, markets and zoo hours on the morning.

Swap to

  • Air-conditioned and free: NGV, ACMI, State Library, local libraries, shopping centres. Melbourne Museum and Scienceworks are free with a concession card.
  • Indoor pools from the Accessible pools list.
  • Early starts. Plan activity for the coolest part of the day and be home by early afternoon.

On the day

  • Carry water, a hat and any medication that must stay cool. Some medications and conditions make heat harder to cope with, so check the person's support plan.
  • Never leave anyone waiting in a parked car.

Planning

Getting there: transport and travel costs

  • Companion Card on public transport: the companion travels free with the cardholder. See the PTV link under Sources.
  • Carer Card: a Carer myki gives half-price travel and free weekend travel in two consecutive zones.
  • Christmas Day and New Year's Eve: in recent years public transport has been free from 3am Christmas Day to 3am Boxing Day, and from 6pm New Year's Eve to 6am New Year's Day. It is announced each December, so check PTV for 2026. Christmas Day runs to a Sunday timetable.
  • Trams: most trams and stops are still not step-free. The state's own accessibility framework says about 75 per cent of tram stops lack raised platforms and about 60 per cent of trams have high floors. Use the PTV journey planner's accessibility filter, and pick a level-access stop at both ends.
  • Multi Purpose Taxi Program: members get half off each taxi fare, up to $60 a trip. It also works on Uber. People who permanently use a wheelchair are exempt from the yearly cap. Phone 1800 638 802.
  • Wheelchair accessible taxis are scarce on Christmas Day, New Year's Eve and during the Australian Open. Book well ahead.

Support

Guide for support workers

Cards to bring

  • Concession card (Pensioner Concession, Health Care or Disability Concession card): gets the participant the concession price. Venues ask to see it at entry, and some want photo ID as well.
  • Companion Card: the support worker's ticket is free at affiliated venues when the participant buys their own ticket. Say you need a companion ticket at the time of booking and show the card on the day. The companion ticket should carry no booking fee, and it still applies when the participant buys a concession ticket.
  • Public transport: with a Companion Card the support worker travels free on trains, trams and buses when travelling with the participant. The participant still needs their own valid ticket.

Who pays

  • As a general guide, the participant pays their own entry and food. Where a venue does not accept the Companion Card, check the participant's plan and your provider's policy before the day to settle who covers the worker's ticket.

Planning the day

  • Book online in advance. It is cheaper at the zoo and aquarium, and the Australian Open Ground Passes are capped this year.
  • On hot days choose indoor venues (NGV, ACMI, museums, aquarium, cinema) or go early. Carry water, hats and sunscreen, and plan shade breaks.
  • For big free events (tree lighting, New Year's Eve, St Kilda Festival, Lunar New Year) arrive early, agree a meeting point, and have an exit plan if the crowd or noise becomes too much. Fireworks and firecrackers are part of several events.
  • Check accessible toilets and Changing Places facilities on the venue's access page before you go, and phone ahead for beach wheelchair bookings.
  • Ask venues for a social story or sensory map. Melbourne Museum, Scienceworks, ACMI and Arts Centre Melbourne all offer them.

Support

Guide for participants and families

Start with what you enjoy

  • Use the By interest and hobby table first, then check the dated calendar for anything happening soon.
  • If crowds or noise are hard, look at Autism and ADHD specific for low sensory sessions, and pick weekday mornings where you can.
  • If money is tight, Top picks and Free all summer list what costs nothing. Melbourne Museum and Scienceworks are free with a concession card.

Cards worth having

  • Companion Card: if you need a support person to take part, their ticket is free at thousands of venues, and they travel free with you on public transport. It is free to apply through the Victorian Companion Card program.
  • Concession card (Pensioner Concession, Health Care or Disability Concession): cheaper entry almost everywhere. Carry it every time.
  • Victorian Carer Card: for unpaid family carers. Discounts, half-price weekday travel and free weekend travel.
  • Multi Purpose Taxi Program: half-price taxi and Uber fares, up to $60 a trip, if you cannot use public transport because of disability.

What to tell your support worker before an outing

  • What you want to get out of the day, and what would make you want to leave.
  • Access needs: wheelchair or mobility aid, Changing Places toilet, hearing or vision support, quiet breaks.
  • Sensory triggers such as fireworks, crowds, bright light or strong smells.
  • Your budget for tickets, food and transport, and which cards you hold.
  • Medication times, heat sensitivity and anything else that affects timing.

Who pays for what

  • As a general guide, you pay for your own ticket, food and travel, the same as anyone else. Your NDIS plan may fund the support worker's time to go with you. Plans differ, so check yours or ask your plan manager or support coordinator.
  • Ask every venue about a Companion Card ticket before paying for your support worker.

Making the day easier

  • Ask the venue for a social story, sensory map or access guide. Melbourne Museum, Scienceworks, ACMI, NGV and Arts Centre Melbourne publish them.
  • Book ahead online. It is usually cheaper and avoids queues.
  • On hot days choose indoor, air-conditioned venues, or go early and finish by lunch.
  • Have a plan B nearby. NGV, ACMI and Fed Square are next to each other, so a change of plan is a short walk.
