In short: practical, recovery-focused support for adults living with mental health conditions, including NDIS participants with psychosocial disability. Led by you, at your pace, by someone who has lived recovery.

Who I support

Adults living with mental health conditions, and NDIS participants with psychosocial disability. People rebuilding after a hard stretch, people managing day to day, and people whose families and coordinators want a steady, respectful support worker in the picture.

What support work can look like

  • Routines and daily living. Building structure that fits your life, from meals and shopping to getting through the week.
  • Getting to appointments. Showing up for the things that matter, with someone beside you.
  • Community and social connection. Getting out again, at a pace that feels safe.
  • Goals you choose. Study, work, relationships, health, whatever recovery means to you.
  • Rebuilding confidence. Small, doable steps that add up.

How I work

Recovery-oriented, trauma-informed and strengths-based. You have choice and control: it is your life and your goals, not mine. I will always be honest and clear about what I can and cannot do, and I will work with your coordinator, family and other providers when you want me to.

Lived experience

I have lived experience of recovery, trauma and mental health challenges, and I work as a support worker in the disability sector. That shapes how I show up: no judgement, no scripts, and no surprise when recovery is not a straight line.

Funding and how it works

Support can be arranged through an NDIS plan or privately. If you are an NDIS participant, ask your support coordinator whether it fits within your plan. Common questions

What is psychosocial disability?

In the NDIS, psychosocial disability means a disability that comes from a mental health condition, such as severe anxiety, depression, PTSD, schizophrenia or bipolar disorder, that affects day-to-day life. It is different from a diagnosis; it is about how the condition affects what you can do.

What does a support worker actually do?

Practical, hands-on support that helps you live the life you want: building routines, daily living tasks, getting to appointments, getting back into community and social activities, and working towards your own goals at your pace.

Is support work the same as counselling?

No. Counselling is talking therapy with a trained counsellor. Support work is practical and day-to-day. They are separate services and you can use either on its own.

Do I need to be on the NDIS?

No. Support can be arranged through an NDIS plan or privately. If you are an NDIS participant, ask your support coordinator whether it fits within your plan.

Can you work with my support coordinator, family or other providers?

Yes, with your consent. Working alongside your existing supports usually gets better results, and you decide who is involved.

Are you a crisis service?

No. If you are in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, call Lifeline on 13 11 14, or 000 in an emergency.

How do I get started?

Send me a short email. We will have a conversation about what you want support with, what funding you have, and whether I am the right person. I will give you written details of how I am set up, including NDIS registration status, before anything starts.
